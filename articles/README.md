## The headline finding

**Mean score 32.25/50. Three of four articles are Marginal, not non-commodity.**

Structure scores 8–9 across the set. Data density scores 7–9. **Experience evidence scores 1–2 on every article**, and that single dimension is what holds all four out of the top band.

A rewrite assembled from secondary sources has a hard ceiling of roughly 35/50, and iteration 1 has reached it. Google's helpful-content systems reward information gain — material a reader cannot get from the pages already ranking. Arranging public facts well is not information gain.

**Iteration 2 must involve doing something, not writing something.**

The highest-return single action: install Aevo MCP, connect it to a client, enumerate all 45 tools, and publish the list with screenshots. Nobody has done this. It would take Article 1 from 32 to roughly 44 and create the only complete reference for that tool surface on the internet.

## Method

**AUF — answer up front.** Every article and every major section leads with the direct answer in short declarative sentences. The reader gets the conclusion before the explanation. No throat-clearing, no "in today's fast-moving world."

**Verifiable claims only.** Every figure is named, dated and sourced. Where a claim rests on a single source it is flagged in `source-verification.md`. Where a topic is inferred rather than confirmed, the article says so in its header.

**FAQs answer real questions.** Eight per article, drawn from gaps the ranking pages leave open — not keyword variants of the headline. Several answer awkwardly: the "11 or 45 tools?" contradiction, whether 271.6% APRs are real, whether a burn guarantees a price rise.

**Honest limitations.** Each article states what it does not know. Article 1 carries a limitations section covering venue size, agent non-determinism and multi-leg fill risk.
