## Important: sourcing constraint

**The original posts could not be read.** `x.com` is blocked by this environment's network egress proxy, as are every mirror tried (`api.fxtwitter.com`, `xcancel.com`, `t.me`), Aevo's own documentation (`docs.aevo.xyz`), and the major crypto data sites (`coinmarketcap.com`).

Each article was therefore **reconstructed from independently verifiable sources** — Aevo's public GitHub repositories, Ondo Finance's own blog, a PRNewswire release, governance documentation, and dated third-party reporting — rather than paraphrased from the posts.

Topic mapping was inferred by decoding each post's timestamp and matching it to Aevo events documented in that window. **Every article carries a confidence rating for that mapping:**

- 01 — **high** (independent reports of the MCP launch dated 8 and 11 March bracket the post)
- 02 — **medium** (one week after Aevo's announced 16 March product unveil; the post itself is a bare media link with no text)
- 03 — **low-to-medium** (independent PERPS+ mobile coverage is dated 19–23 July, about a week earlier)
- 04 — **medium** (two candidate events in the window: the 7 August Ondo listing and the late-August treasury distribution)

Before publishing, confirm each mapping against the actual posts. The product facts stand independently of the mapping; only the "this post said X" framing depends on it.

## Writing methodology applied

**AUF — answer up front.** Every article opens by answering its headline question in short declarative sentences carrying verifiable facts. No throat-clearing, no scene-setting, no "in today's fast-moving world of DeFi."

**Verifiable claims only.** Every number, date and product detail is traceable to a linked source. Where something could not be verified, it is marked unverified rather than filled in with something plausible.

**Analysis labelled as analysis.** The options-equivalence mapping in article 02 is derived reasoning, not a claim Aevo made. It says so.

**Open questions stated as open.** Article 04 raises corporate actions, redemption mechanics and trading-hours mismatch, and says plainly that the sources do not answer them. Admitting a gap is more useful than closing it with a guess — and it is an information-gain signal in itself.

**FAQs answer real questions.** Every FAQ entry is a question a reader would actually type, drawn from the gaps observed in the ranking SERP, not restated from the body copy.

## Iteration log

**Pass 1 — structure.** Source posts rebuilt as articles: AUF opening, H2/H3 hierarchy, FAQ block per article. Scored against the rubric. Result: every article landed in the 40s–50s. Structure alone did not clear the non-commodity threshold, which is the useful negative finding.

**Pass 2 — information gain.** Live SERP research per topic to find what already ranks and what it omits. Added the material nobody else has: the options-structure equivalence table, the stop-loss vs option-floor distinction, MCP key-custody risk, the CCXT/Bitget/Hyperliquid comparison, the position-set table, the 2026 event timeline, and the regulatory and jurisdictional facts every press release leaves out. This pass produced roughly 90% of the score movement.

**Pass 3 — search targeting.** Titles rewritten to target questions rather than brand entities, because brand-name queries lose to price pages (CoinGecko, CoinMarketCap, Kraken, Forbes) on every one of these SERPs. Title tag, meta description and target query added to each article as a front block.

**Pass 4 — not yet possible.** First-hand evidence: screenshots, live premium quotes, observed funding rates. Requires account access. See the per-article "to reach 85+" notes in the scorecard.

## The one-line finding

Structure, headings and FAQs are hygiene — they stop content being penalised. **Information gain is what makes content non-commodity**, and for these four topics the largest remaining gap is first-hand evidence: screenshots, real premium quotes, observed funding rates. That is the next iteration, and it needs the account holder, not the writer.
