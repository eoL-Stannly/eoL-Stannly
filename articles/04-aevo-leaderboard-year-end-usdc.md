# Aevo's Permanent Leaderboard and the 808,800 USDC Year-End Distribution: How Qualification Actually Works

## The answer, up front

Aevo replaced a one-off August payout with a permanent, live leaderboard and moved its
treasury USDC distribution to year-end. The pool grew from roughly **674,000 USDC** to a
projected **808,800 USDC** — about a 20% increase. Qualification needs two things at once:
an active COMMANDER or LEGEND stake on the distribution date, and **$10 million** in
cumulative trading volume since 1 January 2026. Neither alone is enough.

---

## What changed in August 2026

Three changes shipped together.

**1. The August distribution became a year-end distribution.** Roughly 674,000 USDC had
been scheduled for late August 2026. That was superseded by a year-end payout projected at
approximately 808,800 USDC — around 20% more. **[S]**

**2. The leaderboard became permanent.** It is not a campaign. It stays on the platform and
updates in real time as traders trade and stake. **[S]**

**3. Projected rewards became visible before payout.** Every trader can see their live
volume, their staking tier, and their projected year-end USDC reward in their own account,
without waiting for a distribution event. **[S]**

That third change is the substantive one. Most reward programmes reveal your allocation
after the snapshot. Showing a live projection converts a lottery into a target.

## The qualification rules, stated exactly

Two conditions must both be true. **[S]**

| Condition | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Stake | An active **COMMANDER** or **LEGEND** stake on the distribution date |
| Volume | **$10,000,000** cumulative trading volume accrued since **1 January 2026** |

### The staking tiers

| Tier | Lock duration | Granted |
|------|---------------|---------|
| COMMANDER | 8 months | On the day of the lock |
| LEGEND | 12 months | On the day of the lock |

The tier is granted on the day of the lock, not after the lock matures. **[S]** That
detail matters for anyone entering late in the year: locking in November still grants the
tier before a December distribution date, provided the lock is active on that date.

### What the volume threshold implies

$10 million of cumulative volume since 1 January 2026 is a real bar, not a formality. Some
arithmetic to size it:

| Trading cadence | Notional per trade | Trades needed to clear $10M |
|-----------------|--------------------|-----------------------------|
| Daily, one round trip | $50,000 | ~200 trading days |
| Daily, one round trip | $100,000 | ~100 trading days |
| Weekly, one round trip | $250,000 | ~40 weeks |

This is a threshold designed for consistent traders, not for depositors. The programme
pays people who use the exchange, gated by people who commit the token.

## Where the money actually comes from

This is the part most coverage skips, and it is the difference between real yield and
emissions.

The year-end USDC pool is drawn from the protocol treasury's **Uniswap V3 LP fee income**.
**[S]** That income is generated by a mechanic Aevo built into its staking system:

- Aevo's staking system for AEVO went live in **December 2025**. **[S]**
- Staking AEVO grants **sAEVO** — not a separate token or a receipt token, simply the
  staked status of AEVO. **[P]**
- Rewards accrue weekly.
- On the **15th of each month**, accrued AEVO rewards are paired with an equivalent amount
  of USDC and used to mint a **Uniswap V3 AEVO/USDC liquidity position NFT**. **[S]**
- Those positions sit in the **0.3% fee tier**, with liquidity concentrated in a range from
  **50% below to 100% above** the AEVO price at mint. That concentration generates roughly
  **2–3x the fees of a full-range position** while staying active through most realistic
  price movement. **[S]**
- After a vesting period the NFT transfers to the user. **[S]**

So the distribution is fee income from positions the protocol actually holds. The pool
grows or shrinks with real trading activity in the AEVO/USDC pair.

## Three reward streams, stacked

Aevo runs three independent reward structures at the same time. **[S]**

| Stream | Cadence | Funded by | Paid in |
|--------|---------|-----------|---------|
| Trading epoch rewards | Weekly, 1,000,000 AEVO | DAO treasury | AEVO |
| Trading fee cashback | Weekly | Exchange trading fees | USDC |
| Year-end treasury distribution | Annual | Uniswap V3 LP fee income | USDC |

Epoch allocations have moved with product priorities. Epoch 17 (from around 30 March 2026)
split 700,000 AEVO to crypto perp markets and 300,000 AEVO to PERPS+ users. Epoch 20 (from
20 April 2026) split 700,000 AEVO to perps and 300,000 AEVO to options markets. **[S]**

## How this compares to other DEX revenue-share models

| Protocol | Mechanism | Share to holders/stakers |
|----------|-----------|--------------------------|
| Hyperliquid | Fee-funded buybacks | ~90.9% of fees, around $62.6M monthly **[S]** |
| GMX | Direct fee split | 30% to GMX stakers, 70% to the liquidity pool **[S]** |
| Aevo | LP fee income + buyback and burn | Year-end USDC pool, projected ~808,800 for 2026 **[S]** |

The models are not equivalent and the comparison should not be read as a yield ranking.
Hyperliquid's number is large because its volume is large — around $245 billion in 30-day
perp volume mid-2026, against roughly $2.8 billion for GMX in the same window. **[S]**
Aevo's mechanism is structurally different: it converts rewards into productive LP
positions rather than distributing fees directly, so the payout compounds with pool
activity rather than tracking exchange volume one-for-one.

## The supply side: burns and buybacks

The distribution sits alongside a supply-reduction programme authorised by governance
proposal **AGP-3 (Aevonomics)**. **[P]**

- On **9 January 2026**, **69 million AEVO** were burned — **6.9%** of total supply, in a
  one-time event. **[S]**
- AGP-3 introduced ongoing **monthly buybacks and burns** funded from a portion of protocol
  revenue. **[P]**
- The buyback split scales with activity, applying when monthly Aevo trading volume exceeds
  **$500 million**. **[S]**

Two mechanisms therefore run in parallel: USDC flowing out to qualifying stakers, and AEVO
supply being removed. The programme's effectiveness in both directions is tied directly to
trading volume. If volume falls, both the buyback and the LP fee income fall with it. That
is the honest risk in the design, and it is the thing to monitor.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I qualify for Aevo's year-end USDC distribution?
You need both an active COMMANDER or LEGEND stake on the distribution date and $10 million
in cumulative trading volume accrued since 1 January 2026. Meeting only one condition does
not qualify you.

### How much is the Aevo year-end distribution?
Approximately 808,800 USDC is projected for 2026 — about 20% more than the roughly 674,000
USDC originally scheduled for August 2026.

### What is the difference between COMMANDER and LEGEND?
Lock duration. COMMANDER requires an eight-month lock. LEGEND requires twelve months. Both
tiers are granted on the day you lock.

### Do I have to wait for my lock to mature to get the tier?
No. The tier is granted on the day of the lock.

### Where does the USDC come from?
From the protocol treasury's Uniswap V3 LP fee income, generated by AEVO/USDC liquidity
positions minted monthly from accrued staking rewards.

### What is sAEVO?
sAEVO is the staked status of AEVO. It is not a separate token and not a receipt token.

### When are the LP NFTs minted?
On the 15th of each month. Accrued AEVO rewards are paired with an equivalent amount of
USDC and minted as a Uniswap V3 position in the AEVO/USDC 0.3% fee-tier pool.

### Why is the LP range set from −50% to +100%?
Concentrating liquidity in that band produces roughly 2–3x the fees of a full-range
position while remaining active through most realistic price movement. It is a trade
between fee yield and position longevity.

### Is the leaderboard a limited-time campaign?
No. It is a permanent platform feature that updates in real time.

### Can I see my reward before it is paid?
Yes. Live volume, staking tier and projected year-end USDC reward are visible in your own
account.

### Are these rewards token emissions?
The year-end USDC distribution and the weekly USDC cashback are not — they come from LP fee
income and trading fees respectively. The weekly 1,000,000 AEVO epoch distribution is paid
from the DAO treasury.

### How many AEVO have been burned?
69 million AEVO — 6.9% of total supply — were burned on 9 January 2026 under AGP-3, with an
ongoing monthly buyback-and-burn programme running since.

### What is the risk in this model?
Volume dependency. LP fee income, trading-fee cashback and the buyback programme are all
funded by activity on the exchange. A sustained fall in volume reduces all three at once.

2. **Publish the exact distribution date.** "Year-end" is not a date. Find it and state it.
