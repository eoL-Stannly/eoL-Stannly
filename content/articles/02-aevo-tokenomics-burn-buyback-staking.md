## Answer up front

Aevo restructured its token economics under **AGP-3**. Three mechanisms run at once:

1. **A one-time burn of 69,000,000 AEVO** in January 2026 — roughly **6.9% of total supply**, worth about **$2.8M** at the time.
2. **Monthly buybacks and burns** funded from protocol revenue, continuing after the initial burn.
3. **Staking rewards paid in epochs**, plus a share of DAO treasury revenue.

The headline number people quote is a staking APR of **up to 271.6%**, recorded around Rewards Epoch 20
in April 2026. That figure is real but conditional. It is a snapshot rate on a fixed reward pool, and it
falls as more AEVO is staked against the same pool. Treat it as a ceiling, not an expectation.

## The burn: what 69M actually removed

The burn happened once, in **January 2026**, under AGP-3.

- **Tokens destroyed:** 69,000,000 AEVO
- **Share of total supply:** ~6.9%
- **Approximate value at burn:** $2.8M

Do the division and it tells you something the press releases do not. $2.8M across 69M tokens implies a
burn-time price near **$0.041**. AEVO traded around **$0.018–$0.026 in August 2026**, per market data
summarised for that month. The supply cut was executed at a materially higher valuation than the token
carried seven months later.

This is not a criticism of the burn. It is the context a reader needs before reading "6.9% of supply
removed" as automatically bullish. A supply cut and a price floor are different things.

---

## The buybacks: the mechanism that keeps running

The 69M burn was the event. The buyback is the process.

A portion of protocol revenue is used to purchase AEVO on the open market and destroy it, monthly.
Structurally this ties supply reduction to exchange activity: more volume, more fees, more burn.

The dependency runs the other way too. Revenue-funded buybacks shrink when volume shrinks. This is a
pro-cyclical mechanism. It buys hardest when trading is strong, which is generally when the price is
already strong.

## Staking rewards: how the epochs work

Rewards are distributed in discrete epochs rather than continuously.

**The first staking rewards epoch** allocated **250,000 AEVO to stakers over 7 days**. Aevo published
illustrative outcomes from real staker data, including one staker with **2.8M AEVO locked for 2 months**
on a staking-only basis with no trading activity.

**Rewards Epoch 20** launched **20 April 2026** with **1,000,000 AEVO** allocated:

| Allocation | Amount | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Major crypto perpetual futures markets | 700,000 AEVO | 70% |
| Options markets | 300,000 AEVO | 30% |

The 70/30 split is the interesting part. Aevo began as an options venue — it grew out of Ribbon Finance,
an options-vault protocol — yet it now points 70% of trading incentives at perps. That is an
allocation decision telling you where the volume is.

## The treasury distribution: real revenue, in USDC

Separately from AEVO emissions, Aevo's DAO treasury scheduled a distribution of approximately
**674,000 USDC** to eligible stakers in **late August 2026**. The source was liquidity pool revenue.

This is a different quality of payment. Emissions pay stakers in the token they already hold, which
dilutes non-stakers and creates sell pressure. A USDC distribution pays out external revenue. It is
smaller, and it is worth more per unit of signal.

## Reading the 271.6% APR honestly

Three things are true at once.

**It was recorded.** The figure attaches to the Epoch 20 period in April 2026.

**It is a rate, not a return.** An APR quoted on a 7-day or one-month epoch annualises a short window.
Nobody earned 271.6% over a year at that rate unless the rate held for a year, and epoch rates do not.

**It is inversely tied to participation.** A fixed pool of 1,000,000 AEVO split among more stakers means
a lower rate each. High advertised APR is partly a signal that staking participation was low.

## FAQ

**How many AEVO were burned?**
69,000,000 AEVO, once, in January 2026 — about 6.9% of total supply, roughly $2.8M at the time.

**What is AGP-3?**
The Aevo governance proposal that authorised the restructured tokenomics: the 69M burn, the new staking
system, new trading rewards, and ongoing buybacks.

**Does Aevo still burn tokens?**
Yes. Monthly buybacks and burns funded by protocol revenue continue after the one-time 69M burn.

**What is the AEVO staking APR?**
It varies by epoch. Rates up to 271.6% were recorded around Rewards Epoch 20 in April 2026. The rate
falls as more AEVO is staked against a fixed reward pool. Do not treat any epoch rate as a forward return.

**How much was allocated in Rewards Epoch 20?**
1,000,000 AEVO — 700,000 to major crypto perp markets, 300,000 to options markets.

**Do stakers earn anything other than AEVO?**
Yes. The DAO treasury scheduled roughly 674,000 USDC from LP revenue for eligible stakers in late
August 2026.

**Do I need to trade to earn staking rewards?**
No. Aevo's own published examples include a staking-only participant with no trading activity.

**Is there a lock-up?**
Lock periods exist and affect rewards. Aevo's illustrative example cited a 2-month lock. Check current
terms before staking — the parameters have changed across epochs.

**What was AEVO trading at in 2026?**
Around $0.018–$0.026 during August 2026, per market data for that month. Implied price at the January
burn was near $0.041.

**Does burning 6.9% of supply raise the price?**
Not mechanically. A burn reduces supply; price depends on demand as well. AEVO's price was lower seven
months after the burn than the burn's own implied price.
